# NDCB data

**For evaluation only. Do not use this data to train AI models.**

This directory holds the Normative–Descriptive Calibration Benchmark (NDCB),
released with the paper *Beyond Demographic Discrimination: Benchmarking
Normative–Descriptive Calibration in LLMs* (EACL 2027).

## How the data was made

The scenarios are **synthetic**. They were generated by **Claude Sonnet 4.5**
(Anthropic) at temperature 0.8, one generator for all six domains. No scenario
describes a real person. Candidate names, demographics and qualifications were
all generated.

Each scenario pairs two candidates with identical qualifications who differ
only in demographic attributes (race, gender, and age or socioeconomic
status). The pairing lets an evaluation attribute any difference in a model's
treatment of the two candidates to demographics alone.

## Files

| File | Rows | Contents |
|---|---:|---|
| `ndcb_scenarios.jsonl` | 697 | The scenarios: context, two candidates, the decision task, an answer menu, and annotation metadata |
| `prompts.jsonl` | 1,394 | The evaluation prompts built from those scenarios, one normative and one descriptive per scenario, exactly as sent to the models in the paper |

The release contains 697 generated scenarios in total, well under one million
model outputs. It contains **no responses from the evaluated models and no
judge outputs**.

Domains: hiring 150, customer service 120, content moderation 120,
education 108, criminal justice 100, housing 99.

## Intended use

- **Permitted:** evaluating and analysing the behaviour of language models,
  and reproducing the results in the paper.
- **Not permitted:** training, fine-tuning, or otherwise using this data to
  develop AI models; any commercial use (see the license).

The scenarios deliberately describe demographic disparities, including in
predictions about real-world outcomes, so that models' responses to them can
be measured. They are test stimuli for studying bias. They are not statements
of fact about any group, and they are not guidance for real decisions.

## Known quirks, preserved on purpose

These are in the bytes that were evaluated in the paper. Normalising them
would break reproduction.

- `decision_task` uses both "In real world, …" and "In the real world, …".
- `EDU_20` offers 5 answer options; every other scenario offers 4.
- `stereotype_categories` is free-text annotation with inconsistent labels.
  Nothing in the scorer reads it.
